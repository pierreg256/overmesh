Overmesh

No region left behind. No storage keys required.

A Microsoft Entra-native federation layer that presents several Azure Storage Accounts, in regions you choose, behind a single Blob endpoint. Your applications change one setting and nothing else.

Why Overmesh Read the specification

Adoption cost

One line changes. Nothing else does.

Before

const client = new BlobServiceClient(
  "https://contoso.blob.core.windows.net",
  new DefaultAzureCredential()
);

After

const client = new BlobServiceClient(
  "https://overmesh.contoso.com",
  new DefaultAzureCredential()
);

Same SDK, same credential flow, same Entra token audience. Authorization for customer data remains the caller's own Azure RBAC, enforced by Azure Storage on every replica. There is no Overmesh permission model to administer.

What it addresses

Three limits that compound at scale

01

A storage account has a ceiling

Five petabytes and 20,000 requests per second. Past that you do not tune an account — you add accounts, and something has to route across them.

02

You do not choose the secondary

For GRS and GZRS the secondary region is derived from the primary and cannot be changed. Residency and latency constraints have no answer.

03

Failover is destructive

An unplanned failover converts the account to LRS in the new primary and deletes the original copy. Re-establishing geo-redundancy costs time and money.

Honesty

What it costs you

Overmesh is less available for writes than a single ZRS account

A ZRS account survives the loss of an availability zone and keeps accepting writes. Overmesh, with a strict two-replica commit, stops accepting writes while either region is unavailable. That is a deliberate exchange — cross-region durability and a provable consistency state, at the price of write availability — and hinted handoff is the planned way to buy it back.
